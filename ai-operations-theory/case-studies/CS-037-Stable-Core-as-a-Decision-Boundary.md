# CS-037 Stable Core as a Decision Boundary

## Summary

This case records a protocol-design observation that emerged during repeated Repository and Generation revisions.

Preserving a Stable Core did not require freezing old wording or implementation.

Instead, stability referred to protecting a decision boundary from silent loss.

---

## Situation

Protocols changed across Generations.

Wording, document structure, Repository placement, and operational implementation could all be revised.

An ambiguity emerged:

> If a principle is part of the Stable Core, must its historical wording and implementation remain unchanged?

That interpretation would conflict with Reality First when Reality exposed a serious mismatch.

---

## Working Definition

The operational definition became:

> A Stable Core is not old wording or implementation that must remain unchanged. It is a decision boundary that must not disappear implicitly when Generations or Repositories change.

Therefore:

```text
Stable Core
≠
Permanently Frozen Wording
```

```text
Stable Core
≠
Permanent Preservation of an Old Implementation
```

```text
Stable Core
=
Decision Boundary Protected from Silent Loss
```

---

## Explicit Revision

Stable did not mean unchangeable.

Explicit revision remained possible when supported by:

```text
Reality Conflict
+
Human Headquarters Authority
+
Preserved Decision Context
↓
Explicit Revision
```

This allowed the system to preserve continuity without treating historical form as Truth Authority.

---

## Confirmed Observation

Within the observed protocol-maintenance environment:

- stable boundaries could survive wording changes;
- old implementations were not automatically authoritative;
- silent deletion and explicit revision were treated differently; and
- Reality First and continuity could coexist when revision authority and Decision Context were preserved.

---

## Operational Know-how

- Identify the decision boundary being protected.
- Permit wording and implementation to evolve.
- Treat implicit disappearance as a preservation failure.
- Treat explicit, authorized, Reality-grounded revision as a different state.
- Preserve why the boundary existed and why it changed.

---

## Unknown

The observation does not yet establish:

- which decision boundaries qualify as Stable Core;
- who besides Human Headquarters may propose or authorize revision;
- how stability should be audited across large Repository migrations; or
- when accumulated revisions create a new core rather than a modified one.

---

## Generalization Boundary

This definition was developed during AI protocol maintenance.

It may be useful in governance and long-lived knowledge systems, but cross-domain validation remains incomplete.
